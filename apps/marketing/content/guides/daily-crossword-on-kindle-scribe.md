## A pen, a grid, and e-ink — a good match

Solving a crossword on the Scribe is one of the nicer things you can do with the pen:
no glare, no battery anxiety, and you can rest your hand on the screen. The catch is
supply. Amazon sells crossword *books* in the store, but a book is a fixed set of
puzzles you work through once. Most solvers want a *new* puzzle every day, the way it
arrives in a newspaper.

## Getting a puzzle onto the device

The Scribe accepts PDFs by email, so any puzzle you can turn into a PDF you can read
and write on:

- Print-to-PDF a puzzle from a site that offers one, then email it to your
  `@kindle.com` address — after adding your sending address to Amazon's **Approved
  Personal Document E-mail List**.
- Some constructors publish a daily PDF you can download and forward.

Both work; both are a manual step every single day; and you have to keep the answer
key somewhere separate so you don't spoil it.

## What Daily Scribe does

Daily Scribe includes a **write-in crossword** section: a fresh puzzle in every
edition, grid and clues on page one, the answer key on page two so nothing is spoiled
until you flip. It arrives with the rest of your edition at the time you choose, and
because it is a normal PDF you solve it with the pen exactly as you would a printed
grid.

If you also take the news or a Kanji sheet, the crossword rides along in the same
daily digest — one email, one document.

## Solving on the Scribe

- Use a thin pen setting; the default nib can crowd small squares.
- Keep the eraser within reach — the Premium Pen's shortcut button, or the on-screen
  eraser tool.
- Export your finished page from the Scribe's share menu if you want to keep it.
